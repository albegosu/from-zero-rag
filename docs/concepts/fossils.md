# Fossils & Memory

Hypar has no delete operation.

When an idea ends — when it has been superseded, contradicted, exhausted, or simply abandoned — it does not disappear. It becomes a **fossil**: closed, preserved, and part of the long-term memory of the system.

## What a fossil is

A fossil is an embryo that has been closed. It retains everything the embryo was:

- **The original seed** — the raw thought as it was first captured
- **The full lifecycle history** — every state transition, every agent question, every user decision
- **The reason for closure** — always recorded, never optional
- **The connections it held** — which embryos were linked to it at the time of fossilization

Fossils are not archived in the sense of being hidden or inaccessible. They are part of the system's active memory — just not part of its active surface.

## Why fossils exist

The alternative — deletion — destroys information that is often more valuable than the idea itself.

The most important knowledge in a thinking system is not always the ideas that survived. It is:

- **Why certain ideas were abandoned** — the reasoning behind a decision not to pursue something is a decision about something else
- **What was tried and failed** — a fossil with a documented reason for closure is a map of explored territory
- **What resurfaces** — an idea that seemed wrong in one context may become right in another

The agent can say *"you explored this in March and abandoned it because of X"* only because the fossil exists. Without fossils, the system has no past — only a present that keeps repeating itself.

## The geological metaphor

Living embryos exist on the surface — visible, active, engaged. Fossils exist in strata below.

The depth of a fossil is determined by how long ago it was closed and how many living embryos have developed since. Recent fossils are close to the surface. Old ones are deeper.

The agent excavates on your behalf when relevant — surfacing a fossil from whatever depth it occupies when a living embryo connects to it. You can also excavate manually, navigating downward through the strata.

This is not a filing system. You don't browse fossils by category or date. You encounter them when they become relevant — either because the agent surfaces them, or because you choose to dig.

## Fossilization

Fossilization is always a dialogue. Whether initiated by the user or suggested by the agent, it opens a conversation before anything is closed.

### When the user initiates

The agent asks why before closing. The reason the user provides becomes part of the fossil's record. Closing is evaluation: was the problem ill-defined, was the path wrong, or did another idea supersede this one? This is not bureaucratic friction — it is the moment when the most valuable piece of information is captured: *why* the idea ended.

### When the agent suggests

The agent presents its case: the period of inactivity, the contradictions with mature ideas, the reasons it believes the embryo's time has passed. The user decides. Accepting, rejecting, or deferring are all valid responses — all are logged.

### What cannot happen

Fossilization cannot happen silently. An embryo cannot become a fossil without a recorded reason. The system will always prompt for one.

## Resurrection

A fossilized embryo cannot be "un-fossilized" — its lifecycle is complete.

But it can be **resurrected**: a new embryo can be created that explicitly references the fossil. The new embryo starts its own lifecycle, with the fossil as context. The connection between them is permanent — the new embryo knows where it came from, and the fossil knows it was continued.

Resurrection is the system's mechanism for saying: *"That idea was right, but we weren't ready for it then."*
